# Submission guide

Use the issue forms linked from the README. Include an existing result ID
when updating a page. Give primary sources, precise assumptions and theorem,
equation, section or problem numbers where possible. A paper or preprint can
be reported without treating its claims as established.

Credit original authors, problem contributors and reviewers. Report AI
involvement only when disclosed by a source, with a locator. Identify the
proof version a review or explanation concerns, and distinguish independent
review, author checking and editing. Lean links and documentation are enough;
do not run surveyed code.

You may suggest an original conjecture: identify it as your proposal and supply
background references. If it already appears in the literature, give the
original attribution. Suggestions do not establish scholarly priority by the Map.

Your issue and GitHub author identity are public immediately. Do not post
private contact details or confidential material. Public credit is optional;
you can request a display name or no acknowledgment, but this does not hide
your GitHub identity on the issue.

Submit your own wording under the repository's Apache 2.0 license, with
permission to share it. Identify third-party quotations and their source terms;
link to papers rather than reproducing them.

Discuss evidence and scope respectfully. Maintainers may close spam, duplicates
and off-topic submissions, and moderate abusive content. No response deadline
is promised. Closing an issue is an editorial action, not a verdict on proof
correctness.
